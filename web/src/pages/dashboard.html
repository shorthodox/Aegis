Risk & Capital Management

How to size positions, protect your capital, and use AEGIS signals profitably
Save and re-read this regularly. These rules are what separate traders who last for years from traders who blow up in weeks. The signals tell you what to trade — this guide tells you how much and how safely.

The Five Golden Rules — Memorise These

1–2%
Maximum risk per trade, never more
1:1.5
Minimum reward:risk on every trade
70%+
Minimum AI confidence to trade real money
5%
Max total risk across all open positions
10%
Daily drawdown limit — stop trading for the day

What AEGIS Refuses To Trade — Automatic Guardrails

These are enforced by the engine before a signal is published — you do not have to check them. They exist because each one was measured against real losing trades. Knowing what is already filtered tells you what is left for you to judge.

45/100 Signal quality floor

A setup scoring below 45 is not traded, however confident the model is. The score weighs trend, momentum, volume, regime confidence, funding, open interest, higher-timeframe context and candle structure. Previously nothing enforced this and a signal scoring 0/100 could hold a slot.

1 ATR Must be at its level

A BUY must sit within 1 ATR of the support it leans on, a SELL within 1 ATR of its resistance. Entries taken mid-range — where the setup's own premise is absent — are refused. If price is further out but still in reach, the signal becomes a working order at the level that expires after 8 bars rather than chasing.

0.50–1.30% Stop distance band

Every stop is placed beyond the level that invalidates the trade, then bounded to this band as a risk budget. A stop tighter than 0.50% sits inside normal noise and gets taken out by a wick; wider than 1.30% and the loss is too large for the ladder to pay back.

1:1.6 Net R:R after costs

Measured net — fees and spread come off the win and are added to the loss, the way they actually land. The objective must be a real structural level at least 1.5 ATR away, not a projection. A setup that only pays on paper is rejected at this stage.

5 · 2 Book & cluster caps

At most 5 positions open at once, and at most 2 in the same correlated group. Eight alt shorts opened in one hour is one bet wearing eight tickets — that basket is exactly what this cap exists to stop. Counter-tide trades are also sized down by half.

TP1 → BE Break-even ratchet

The moment TP1 (1.5%) is tagged, a partial is banked and the stop moves to your entry price. From that point the trade cannot lose. If you are following signals manually, move your stop to entry when TP1 fills — this is the single highest-value habit on this page.

Signal Quality vs AI Confidence — They Are Not The Same Number

Every card shows both, and they often disagree. That is not a bug — they answer different questions, and knowing which is which changes how you size.

Signal Quality /100

How good is the setup? An absolute reading of the market context — trend, volume, regime, funding, higher timeframes, candles. Comparable across tokens and across days. This is the one with a hard floor at 45.

AI Confidence %

How unusual is this bar for this token? A percentile rank of the model's edge against its own recent history. It is relative: in a quiet stretch an ordinary bar can rank high. Useful for timing, but it says nothing absolute about setup quality — which is why it is not the floor.

Rule 3 above — 70%+ AI confidence for real money — is still your bar, on top of the engine's. The engine will publish a quality-50 signal; whether you take it with real capital is your call, and holding out for both a high quality score and high confidence is the conservative choice.

About Signals Tagged RISKY

A RISKY tag means the engine took the trade but flagged it — thinner payoff, a weaker location, or a counter-trend thesis. The portfolio published on our track record does include RISKY-tier signals. Treat them with your own analysis and trade them on paper first. They are not a lower-conviction version of the same trade; they are a different risk profile, and sizing them like a STRONG signal is how a good month turns into a bad one.

Mean-reversion setups — fading a move back into a level — are scored on their own merits rather than penalised for being counter-trend, so a genuine reversal at a level can now clear the quality floor. Counter-trend trades remain the harder half of the book: size them smaller than trend-following entries.

Position Sizing Formula — Step by Step

The Formula
Position Size = (Account × Risk%) ÷ (Entry Price − SL Price)
The result is the number of units / coins to buy or sell
Step 1
Calculate Your Risk $
Multiply your account size by your chosen risk %. Use 1% if you're a beginner.
$1,000 × 1% = $10
Step 2
Calculate SL Distance
Subtract the SL price from the entry price shown on the signal card.
$50.00 − $47.50 = $2.50
Step 3
Divide to Get Units
Divide your risk $ by the SL distance. This is how many units to buy.
$10 ÷ $2.50 = 4 units
Worked Examples

$1,000 1% $10 $50.00 $47.50 $2.50 4 units
$2,000 1.5% $30 $0.20 $0.18 $0.02 1,500 units
$5,000 2% $100 $28,000 $26,600 $1,400 0.071 BTC

Risk:Reward — Why the Math Matters

You don't need to win every trade. You need to win enough — and that number depends on your R:R ratio.

Break-Even Win Rate by R:R
R:R Ratio Min Win Rate Example: 10 trades
1 : 1 51% 6 wins, 4 losses → +$20
1 : 1.5 40% 4 wins, 6 losses → +$0 (break even)
1 : 2 34% 4 wins, 6 losses → +$2 profit
1 : 3 26% 3 wins, 7 losses → +$2 profit

AEGIS signals target 1:1.5 to 1:2. The cyan row is the minimum you should accept.

How to read it

R:R 1:2 means for every $1 you risk, you aim to make $2. If your SL is $5 away from entry, your TP should be at least $10 away.

With a 1:2 ratio, even if you lose 6 out of 10 trades, you still come out profitable: 4 wins × $20 = $80 gained, 6 losses × $10 = $60 lost → +$20 net.

This is why cutting losers fast and letting winners run is the core principle of every successful trader.

Capital Management Rules — Apply Every Day

1
Total open risk ≤ 5% at all times
If you have 3 open trades each risking 2%, that's 6% total — too much. Reduce position size or skip the third trade.
2
Stop trading after a 10% daily drawdown
If your account is down 10% in a single day, close everything and stop. Emotional trading after big losses makes it worse, not better.
3
After 3 losses in a row, halve your risk
Drop from 2% to 1%, or from 1% to 0.5%. This limits damage during bad runs and keeps you in the game until conditions improve.
4
Take partial profit at 50% of TP
When price reaches halfway to your TP, close 50% of the position and move SL to break-even. You lock in some profit while letting the rest run.
5
Reinvest profits — don't raise risk %
If your account grows from $1,000 to $1,200, keep risking 1% ($12 per trade, not $20). Let compounding do the work gradually, not forced risk.
6
Review every 20 trades — not every trade
A single trade tells you nothing about your edge. 20 trades gives you a meaningful sample. Review win rate, average R:R, and emotional discipline across the set.

Common Mistakes That Kill Accounts — and How to Avoid Them

FOMO — Chasing Missed Signals
Entering after the price has already moved significantly from the entry zone. You get the same risk but less reward. Fix: if price is more than 0.5% past entry, skip it. The next signal is minutes away.
Revenge Trading
Taking a bigger trade to "win back" a loss. This almost always makes losses larger. Fix: after a loss, take a 10-minute break. Come back only when calm.
Moving Your Stop Loss Further Away
Hoping the price comes back by giving it "more room." Planned small losses become catastrophic unplanned ones. Fix: SL is sacred. Set it and do not touch it.
Over-Leveraging on Futures
Using 10×, 20× or 50× leverage on signals designed for spot or low leverage. A 2% move against you wipes your position. Fix: beginners use 1–3× max. No more.
Trading With Money You Can't Afford to Lose
Rent money, savings, borrowed money. Emotional pressure from needing the money to survive causes poor decisions. Fix: only trade with money you've mentally written off already.
Taking Every Signal Without Filtering
Not all signals are equal. Entering on weak confluence or low AI confidence turns a quality system into gambling. Fix: use the pre-trade checklist before every single trade.

Rule 1 · Risk

Never Risk More Than 1–2% of Your Account Per Trade

Protect your capital so losing streaks don't wipe you out

Even the best signals lose sometimes. The key is to stay in the game. Risk only 1–2% of your total balance on any single trade.

Formula: Position Size = (Account × 1%) ÷ (Entry Price − SL Price)

Example: $500 account → risk $5 per trade. If Entry = $1.00 and SL = $0.95, that's a $0.05 gap → buy 100 units ($5 ÷ $0.05). You can only lose $5, no matter what.

Rule 2 · Discipline

Set Your Stop Loss the Moment You Enter — and Never Move It

The stop loss is your only protection — don't switch it off

Every AEGIS signal includes a pre-calculated Stop Loss (SL) price. The moment your entry fills, immediately place a stop-loss order at that SL price.

If price reaches your SL — exit, no questions asked. Never move the SL further away hoping the trade recovers. Doing so turns a small, planned loss into a large, unplanned one. The signal was wrong; cut it and move on.

Rule 3 · Patience

Enter Near the Signal Price — or Skip the Trade Entirely

A late entry shrinks your profit and keeps your full risk on the table

Each signal has an Entry Zone. If the price has already moved significantly past that zone by the time you see it, skip this trade. Entering late means you take the same SL distance but less upside — a worse deal.

Practical rule: if the current price is more than 0.5% beyond the entry price shown, wait for the next signal. There is always another one.

Rule 4 · Simplicity

One or Two Trades at a Time While You're Learning

Fewer positions means clearer thinking and fewer mistakes

When you're new, having many open trades feels exciting — but it's hard to manage and easy to make errors under pressure. Stick to one or two open positions at a time until every step (entry, SL, TP, sizing) feels automatic.

Once managing a single trade is easy — sizing, monitoring, and exiting cleanly — you can gradually expand. Slow is smooth; smooth is fast.

Pre-Trade Checklist

COMPLETE ALL STEPS BEFORE EXECUTING ANY TRADE

1

Cross-Check the Confluence Score

Click the signal card and look at the Confluence bar. It shows how many indicators agree with the signal direction. A higher score = stronger signal — more systems are pointing the same way. If the bar is weak or split, skip this trade and wait for a stronger setup.

2

Verify Your Stop Loss & Take Profit

Check the SL (Stop Loss) and TP (Take Profit) prices shown on the card. Make sure the TP is meaningfully further from entry than the SL — aim for at least 1.5× reward vs risk (e.g., risk $10 to make $15+). If the SL looks very far away, your position size must be smaller to keep risk at 1–2%.

3

Confirm Signal Strength Before Entering

Check the AI Confidence % on the card. Signals above 70% are higher quality. Between 55–70% is moderate — consider paper trading. Below 55%, skip entirely or practice only. Never enter a trade just because a signal appeared — it must also be strong.

4

Only Then — Execute the Trade

Go to the Terminal, set your risk at 1–2% of your account, and place your entry, SL, and TP. If you're still building confidence, use TradingView paper trading instead of real money. Once all three checks above are green, execute.
