Under The Hood

How a signal is built.

Nine sequential gates separate a candidate signal from a live one. Most candidates are vetoed. Only setups that pass every layer get through.

01
Market Regime Detection

Before any signal is evaluated, AEGIS classifies the market regime — trending, ranging, volatile, or reversal. Signals are suppressed in regimes where the model's historical edge is below threshold. If BTC is chopping sideways, the engine mostly stays quiet.

02
XGBoost Ensemble Scoring

An XGBoost model trained on 60+ structural features — OHLCV, EMA slopes, RSI momentum, volume Z-score, ATR normalisation, VWAP deviation, funding rate alignment, and more — produces a directional probability and an edge score. Scores below 65 are rejected outright.

03
Contextual Quality Filter

A secondary scorer evaluates the signal's environmental context: ADX strength, volume confirmation, regime confidence, macro BTC/ETH bias alignment, funding rate and open interest divergence. Weak context scores below 55 are blocked here — even if the XGBoost edge is high.

04
RSI Exhaustion & ATR Floor

Late entries at momentum peaks are blocked by the RSI exhaustion gate: no BUY when RSI ≥ 72 with declining slope, no SELL when RSI ≤ 28 with recovering slope. The ATR floor ensures the market is moving enough to justify execution costs.

05
Portfolio Guard & Position Sizing

The final gate evaluates portfolio exposure: maximum open positions, sector concentration, and correlation across concurrent signals. Only then are TP and stop-loss levels calculated using triple-barrier labellingTriple-Barrier Labels
Exit levels are set by ATR multiples, not fixed percentages — so targets adapt to real current volatility, not historical averages. based on real current ATR.
